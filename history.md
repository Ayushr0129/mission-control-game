## Historical Facts and Events

Working research list for the NASA Space Apps Challenge. Split into two questions:
what made spaceflight *possible* (enablers) and what *advanced* it (capabilities and firsts).

---

## The causal chain in ten steps

1. **Physics proven possible** — Newton's laws and gravitation (1687); Tsiolkovsky's rocket equation (1903).
2. **Vacuum thrust verified** — Goddard's 1919 paper and 1926 flight proved a rocket needs no air to push against.
3. **Orbital mechanics solved** — Oberth (1923) and the Hohmann transfer (1925) gave a minimum-fuel route to other planets.
4. **Big rockets built** — the V-2 (1942) and its ICBM descendants (R-7, Atlas, Redstone) became the first launchers.
5. **A reason to pay for it** — Sputnik (1957) plus Cold War competition unlocked sustained funding.
6. **Institutional machinery** — NASA (1958), the Deep Space Network (1958), launch ranges, tracking and test facilities.
7. **Humans shown to survive it** — Mercury, Vostok and Gemini established life support, EVA, rendezvous and docking.
8. **Heavy lift and precision guidance** — Saturn V, the Apollo Guidance Computer, Apollo 8 and Apollo 11.
9. **Persistence in space** — stations (Salyut 1971, Mir 1986, ISS 1998–2000) and the reusable Shuttle (1981).
10. **Cost collapse and open access** — commercial launch and reuse (Falcon 9 2010, booster landing 2015), smallsats, rideshare.

## Phase 1 — Theory: making it possible on paper (1687–1925)

| Date | Event | Why it was a step forward |
|---|---|---|
| 1687 | Newton, *Principia* | Laws of motion and gravity; orbital flight becomes derivable, not mystical |
| 1898 | Tsiolkovsky proposes rockets for spaceflight | Reframed exploration as engineering rather than fantasy |
| 1903 | Tsiolkovsky, *Exploration of the Universe with Rocket-Propelled Vehicles* | Derives the rocket equation — delta-v budgeting, liquid fuels, staging |
| 1919 | Goddard, *A Method of Reaching Extreme Altitudes* | Proved a rocket works in a vacuum (no air to react against) |
| 1923 | Oberth, *Die Rakete zu den Planetenräumen* | Popularised multi-stage rockets and human spaceflight |
| 1925 | Hohmann transfer orbits | The practical method for reaching another planet on minimum fuel |

## Phase 2 — Hardware: rockets that leave the ground (1926–1957)

| Date | Event | Why it was a step forward |
|---|---|---|
| 1926 | Goddard's liquid-fuel rocket (2.5 s, 12.5 m, 56 m) | Ancestor of every liquid-propellant launcher, incl. Saturn V |
| 1927 | Verein für Raumschiffahrt founded | Pooled engineering talent; nucleus of the Peenemünde team |
| 1942 | V-2 first successful flight | First mass-produced guided rocket; basis of US and Soviet launchers |
| 1944 | V-2 vertical test (~189 km) | First human-made object to reach space |
| 1945 | Operation Paperclip / Soviet seizure of Peenemünde | Transferred rocket expertise to both superpowers |
| 1947 | Fruit flies launched on a V-2 | First biological payload recovered from space |
| 1948 | Bumper-WAC two-stage flight | First two-stage rocket; upper stage reached ~393 km |
| 1957 | R-7 ICBM test flights | Missile technology becomes launch technology |

## Cross-cutting enablers

The technologies that recur across every phase — useful for framing a solution.

- **Guidance, navigation and control:** gyroscopes (1920s) → Apollo Guidance Computer (1969) → star trackers → terrain-relative navigation → autonomous flight software.
- **Propulsion:** liquid hydrogen/LOX upper stages → hypergolics → solid boosters → ion and Hall electric propulsion → nuclear thermal (NERVA tested, never flown) → methalox full-flow staged combustion.
- **Materials and thermal protection:** ablative heat shields → reinforced carbon-carbon → PICA → aerogels → deployable composites.
- **Power:** solar cells (1958) → RTGs (1961) → lithium-ion → large deployable arrays.
- **Communications and navigation:** Deep Space Network (1958) → TDRSS → Ka-band → optical comms → GPS and LEO PNT.
- **Life support:** pressure suits → environmental control and life support systems → regenerative CO₂ scrubbing → closed-loop water → radiation shielding.
- **Landing technology:** retropropulsion → airbags → sky crane → hazard avoidance → reusable boosters with landing legs and grid fins.
- **Autonomy and software:** ground commanding → onboard flight software → AI/ML rover navigation (AEGIS, Perseverance AutoNav).
- **Manufacturing and servicing:** in-space 3D printing, on-orbit servicing (Hubble SM1–SM4, MEV, robotic refuelling).

## Institutional and policy steps

Often left out of timelines but decisive in practice.

- 1957–58 Sputnik shock → national space acts, funding agencies and science advisory structures.
- 1967 Outer Space Treaty — the legal frame that makes exploration permissible and non-sovereign.
- 1970s–80s technology transfer programmes → a commercial satellite industry.
- 1993 ISS partnership agreement — multilateral pooling of modules and budgets.
- 2006–2015 commercial cargo and crew (COTS, CRS, CCDev) — government buys services instead of vehicles.
- 2015 US Commercial Space Launch Competitiveness Act — property rights over extracted resources.
- 2020 Artemis Accords — norms for lunar and planetary activity.
- Ongoing: spectrum allocation, orbital-debris mitigation (25-year rule tightening to 5), export-control (ITAR) reform.
